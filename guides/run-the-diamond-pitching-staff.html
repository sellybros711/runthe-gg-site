Building a pitching staff that survives October

A guide to Run The Diamond

In Run The Diamond you draft an all-time roster under a salary cap, play 162 games, then chase a ring. A great regular season doesn't guarantee one. This guide is about the arms: how the game sets your staff, what October does to it, and where your money goes furthest.

The cap

You get $190M for twelve spots, in every draft mode. Prices come from WAR, so the best arms cost the most. The budget always holds back at least $1M for each open spot. A re-spin costs $5M, then $10M, then $15M, and you get three.

A starter who threw more than 210 innings is priced and rated on what he did in 210. His card says what he counts as. So a deadball workhorse isn't a bargain. You pay for what you get.

Classic: two starters and a closer

The Classic draft has three pitching spots: SP1, SP2 and CL.

  • Your two starters cover 45% of the innings. A league-average arm pitches the rest.
  • SP1 and SP2 are one job. A starter goes straight in. There's no choice to make.
  • The closer decides close games. Lead by 3 or fewer and he can blow it. An average closer saves 80%. Each win of WAR adds 2 points, up to 95%. That rate plays in October too.

All-Time Pitching Staff: twelve arms

You draft twelve arms: a five-man rotation (SP1 to SP5), a six-man pen (RP1 to RP5 and SU) and a closer (CL). Your lineup is league average. The season turns on the runs you give up.

The staff assigns itself. You never pick a slot. After every pick the game re-ranks the twelve:

  • The rotation takes your best arms. The five starters throw 70% of the innings. That's 14% a rotation slot against about 4% a relief slot. SP1 is your ace.
  • CL goes to your best closer-eligible arm. It's the one relief slot read by name, for the save rate.
  • Everyone else fills the pen.

A starter can work out of the pen. A reliever can't start, and a starter can't close. So CL needs a real reliever or closer.

Inside a group, the order doesn't change your season. The starters are averaged together, and so is the pen. It does change October: Game 1 of a series goes to SP1, Game 2 to SP2, and on down. A Pro player can swap arms inside a group before the first pitch.

Two-spot players

A two-way season is two cards: one for the bat, one for the arm. Each carries half the year.

A hitter who fits two open spots asks where he goes. Every spot he can take glows gold on the field. Tap one to sign him there. A pitcher never asks, in either mode.

How you reach October

  • 95 wins wins the division. You skip the Wild Card round and play 3 rounds.
  • 88 wins gets a wild card. You play 4 rounds.
  • Below 88, the season's over.

The Wild Card and Division Series are best of 5. The last two rounds are best of 7.

Why October gets harder

Every round is tougher. In your second round, the other side scores 15% more than in your first. In your third, 30% more. In a fourth, 45%. A division winner tops out at 30%. A wild card has to win at 45%.

A weak team gets a stiffer fight late. Below a rating of 90, the World Series opponent gets tougher the further under you are. The Championship Series gets half of that. In Classic, a team rated 85 or better gets an easier October. In the Pitching Staff mode, the rating is the staff's own ERA scale: a 4.63 ERA rates 1 and a 2.77 rates 99.

So a ring means you built a great staff, not that you got hot.

Making the calls in October

In the October games you watch, a Pro player can pull the pitcher and bring in the next one. The call is real. It decides the rest of that game, and October carries on from it. In Classic and the daily, that's the deciding game only. Free players watch the coach make the same calls.

The coach's starter goes six. From the seventh, a reliever takes over. In Classic nobody else was drafted, so your starter stays on, and the call sheet offers a league-average bullpen arm. The closer comes in for the ninth with a lead of 1 to 3. Picking the coach's own arm changes nothing.

What you're managing:

  • Pitch count. It's shown beside "Pitching". Past 95 a man gets hittable, worse with every pitch.
  • Tired arms. A reliever who threw over 25 pitches or got 4 outs is tired the next game of that series. Yesterday's starter used in relief is more tired still. Nothing carries past a series.
  • The stops. The game asks at 95, 110 and 125 pitches, when the coach wants a change, for a save in the 8th or 9th, and for the tying run from the 7th on. The Mound button changes pitchers between any two batters.

Tips

  • Spend on the rotation. In the Pitching Staff mode, a rotation slot carries over three times the innings of a relief slot.
  • Get one real ace. The re-rank pays when there's a gap at the top. Twelve similar arms are the same staff in any order.
  • Take the best arm you can afford. In the Pitching Staff mode, holding money back doesn't beat that.
  • Don't skimp on the closer. A better one saves more close games, all year and in October.
  • Chase 95 wins. The bye skips a round and the hardest step of the climb.
  • In Classic, pay for your starters. They throw 45% of your innings.
  • As a Pro, use your best reliever, then rest him a game. A tired arm gives it back.
