Winning a Full Team run: coaches and fourth downs

A guide to The Perfect Season

Full Team is the biggest draft in The Perfect Season. You sign twelve men instead of six: a whole offense and a whole defense, out of one salary cap. Then you pick a coach, or you don't. This guide answers one question: what do your calls during a run actually change? Short answer: more than you'd think, and most of it happens in January.

Getting in

Full Team is open to everybody with a free RunThe.GG account and a username. A free account gets one run a day. The day turns over at midnight Eastern.

The run is counted when you kick off the season, not when you start the draft. A run you've started never gets locked out. The door always says Resume, whatever day it is. The Perfect Season's premium plan takes the daily limit off.

Building the team

The draft works like the quick game. A wheel lands on a year, then a team from that year. You sign one man off it. Don't like the draw? Re-spin it for $5M, then $10M, then $15M. That's three re-spins at most, and the fees come out of your cap.

The difference is the sides. Picks alternate: offense first, then defense, then offense again, six a side. The field lights up the half you're picking for.

  • Offense: QB, RB, two WRs, TE and a FLEX that takes an RB, WR or TE.
  • Defense: two DL, LB, two DBs and a FLEX that takes a DL, LB or DB.

You get $280M for all twelve. Your coach comes out of that same money. If the screen tells you to keep some back, listen. It means you're close to not affording the spots you have left.

What the ratings mean

Each side gets its own rating. Offense is what your six score. Defense is what your six stop. Each side has its own chemistry and its own fit, so a great defense can't fix a broken offense.

The team overall is built from the two. It sits on the same scale as the rest of the game:

  • 84 is what you get for spending the whole cap with care. That's where a careful six man draft lands too.
  • 100 is the best roster the mode can build.

That number isn't just for show. The game reads it every week. A higher overall wins more ordinary Sundays. It decides how much home field you get in the playoffs. A team rated 100 or better gets the first round off with only 13 wins. The results screen shows every step from your men to the overall, so you can check it.

Choosing a coach

After the draft you hire a coach with the money you have left. Each coach shows one number: what he'd do to your rating. The best one sits at the top.

Coaches who'd make your team worse aren't shown. The money left after the draft can't buy anything else, so paying for a man who lowers the rating is always worse than going without. A coach can show +0.0 and still matter. He might lift your offense and drop your defense by the same amount.

A coach also brings a game plan: tempo, pressure and how he treats fourth down. "Knows 2" on his card means he's linked to two of your men, and that adds chemistry.

If you spent the whole cap, you may find nobody worth hiring. The screen says so. That's a good draft, not a bad one.

Tap a coach to open his sheet. Then hire him, or press "No coach, I'll call it myself".

The regular season

All 17 games play themselves. Nobody stops for a call. With a coach, his plan runs all season. Without one, the team plays a neutral plan.

Win 12 and you're in the playoffs as a wild card, with four games to the title. Win 16 and you're the top seed, with three. One playoff loss ends the run.

The playoffs: where your calls happen

Every Full Team playoff game is played live, drive by drive. It stops for two things: a real fourth down, and the two point try. Whoever coaches the team answers.

No coach: you get the buttons. The game stops on a fourth down when:

  • it's 3 yards or less to go, at their 48 or closer,
  • you're behind late in the game, at your own 35 or further,
  • or you're behind in overtime.

You choose Go for it, or the safe option. That's a field goal if you're in range (the button says how long) and a punt if you're not. Every other fourth down the game handles for you.

The two point try comes up from the second half on, when the game is within 10. Go for two or kick the point. Two works between 30% and 60% of the time, and a stronger offense sits higher.

With a coach: he makes the same calls by a fixed rule, and the screen says what he chose before it plays out. His rule:

  • Go for it: goes on 4th and 3 or less.
  • Standard: goes on 4th and 2 or less.
  • Punt it: never goes.
  • Any coach goes for it when he's down more than 3 in the fourth quarter.
  • In the fourth quarter he goes for two at these margins, counted after the touchdown: down 10, down 5, down 2, up 1, up 4 or up 5. An aggressive coach also goes for two to tie, from the second half on.

Every call goes in the drive log, his or yours, so you can scroll back and check him.

Tips

  • Spend the cap. Money you don't use buys nothing. That's what puts you at 84.
  • Keep both sides honest. Picks alternate for a reason. Blow it all on six stars and the other six have nothing left.
  • Hire for the number, not the name. The rating change on his card is the whole case for him.
  • Go for it on short fourth downs. In the game's own testing, going for it more often won more playoff games than playing safe. Calling it yourself has the highest ceiling of all.
  • Learn the two point chart. Down 2 after a late touchdown? Go for two. It ties the game.
  • Use your one run well. Your free run is counted at kickoff. Take your time in the draft.
