The Perfect Season is the NFL game on RunThe.GG. You draft six players from real seasons, then play a full season and the playoffs. Go unbeaten and you finish 20-0. Most runs are won or lost before a game is played, in how you spend the cap. This guide covers how that money works and how to use it.

## How the draft works

The wheel lands on a year, then on a team from that year. You sign one player off that team's roster. Six spins fill six spots:

- QB
- RB
- WR
- WR
- TE
- FLEX (a running back, receiver or tight end)

Each card is priced and scored from that player's real stats in that one season. Not his career. A legend in a down year costs a lot and gives you little. A role player in his one big year can be the best buy on the board.

A player who plays two positions gets asked where he plays. Pick the spot that leaves your later picks the most room.

## The cap and what's left

You get **$140M** for all six spots. Players cost from **$3M** to **$48M**.

The game won't let you strand yourself. It keeps back **$3M** for every spot you still have to fill after this one. A player who would eat into that shows greyed out. So on your first pick you can never spend more than $125M. You don't need to do that math yourself. Just know the board already did it.

If a spin lands on a team with nobody you can afford at an open spot, it spins again for free. You're never charged for a dead draw.

## Re-spins: New year and New team

Don't like the draw? You get two buttons:

- **New team** keeps the year and lands on a different club from it.
- **New year** keeps the club and lands on a different season of it.

Both cost the same. The price climbs with each one you take, whichever button you press:

- First re-spin: **$5M**
- Second: **$10M**
- Third: **$15M**

That's **3** re-spins a draft, and **$30M** if you take all of them. The fee comes off the same cap that signs your players. A re-spin that would leave too little to fill your roster is blocked.

In One Franchise mode the team never changes, so you only get New year.

## How team strength becomes a record

Your team overall is your six players' points per game, multiplied by two things.

**Chemistry.** Players who share something lift the whole roster. A quarterback and a receiver he really threw to is the strongest link. Teammates from the same season, the same franchise, the same college or the same draft class count too. Links stack but each one adds a bit less than the last. The total tops out at **15%**.

**Shape.** The game checks that your six make a real offense:

- **Your quarterback feeds your catchers.** A weak passer shrinks what your receivers and tight end are worth.
- **Balance.** About **a quarter** of your non-passing points should come on the ground. Far off that and you're easy to defend.
- **Don't lean on one man.** If your best player is much more than **a quarter** of your total, defenses key on him.
- **No empty jerseys.** Your two weakest players should average at least **half** of your team average. Two stars and four bargain bodies fail this one hard.

Then you play **17** games against real teams from the pool. Each week every player has a good or bad day around his average. A stronger overall gets a small extra edge each Sunday from about **90**.

## The playoffs

Your record decides your seed:

- **12 wins** gets you in as a wild card. That's **4** games to the title.
- **16 wins** gets the top seed and a bye. That's **3** games.
- A roster rated **100** or more earns the bye at **13 wins**.

One playoff loss ends the run. The last two games are against the 2007 Patriots and then the 1972 Dolphins. The Super Bowl reads your roster, not your record. Around a **95** overall it's about an even game. Below that you're the underdog, and it gets worse fast under **90**.

So 17-0 isn't enough. You need a roster good enough to beat the best teams ever in a single game.

## Cap strategy that works

- **Spend it all.** Money left at the end is points you never put on the field. Aim to finish with almost nothing.
- **Two stars, not three.** The cap fits two top-priced players. A third leaves you with empty jerseys, and the shape check punishes that.
- **Buy the quarterback first, or plan for him.** Every catcher you sign is worth more with a real passer throwing to him.
- **Spread the middle.** Two players at $48M leave $44M for four spots. That's about $11M a man. Check what the middle of the board costs before you sign the second star.
- **Chase chemistry on purpose.** If you have a quarterback, look on later spins for receivers who caught passes from him. Two linked mid-priced players often beat one star with no links.
- **Re-spin early and once.** The first re-spin is cheap. The third costs as much as a decent starter. Use them on a bad draw at a spot you can't fill well, not to chase a slightly better name.
- **Read the year.** Ask what that player really did that season before you pay for the name.
- **Watch the overall.** Below about 90, the playoffs will likely end your run. Around 95, the final is a fair fight.
