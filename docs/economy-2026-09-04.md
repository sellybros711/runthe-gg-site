# The RunTheRopes economy, ported from Run The Tour, 2026-09-04

What the golf game does, what the wrestling game was doing instead, and what
changed. Written down because the numbers here are the whole design, and a
number nobody can find the reasoning for gets "adjusted" by the next person
who looks at it.

---

## The thing that was broken

Coins were the purse. `applyMatch` paid the match purse straight into
`G.bag.coins`, so the store currency scaled with the career.

Measured, not guessed. A headless 12-year career:

| | |
|---|---|
| coins earned | **2,881,039** |
| every locked item in the catalogue, bought outright | **1,650,300** |
| what that ratio means | one career buys the entire game **1.7 times over** |

By year nine a single match was paying about 25,000 coins, against a
legendary cosmetic priced at 30,000. One match, one legendary. There was
nothing to grind toward because the grind finished before the player ever
opened the store.

Run The Tour had the same problem and fixed it the same way, which is the
reason it is worth copying rather than inventing: golf's comment on
`COIN_RATE` says a $1.99 coin bucket should be worth about one full 30-year
career's earnings. That only works if a career is worth a small number.

## The fix: two currencies that are not the same thing

**Money ($)** is kayfabe. The purse, the weekly downside, the merch cut, the
season bonus. It pays for the life, it is what a contract is worth, and it
stays career-scaled. A main-eventer clears six figures a night and should.

**Coins** are the meta currency. They buy gear across every career the player
will ever run, so they are slow and they do not scale with the purse.

Every coin now comes through one function, `awardCoins()`, which is also
where the pass XP, the daily cap and any future multiplier hook in. One
chokepoint, on purpose.

### The faucet

| source | coins |
|---|---|
| a match, by card position | 22 opener, 38 mid-card, 65 main event |
| supercard | x1.6 |
| match quality | +15 at 64, +35 at 72, +70 at 80, +140 at 88 |
| winning a title | 350 |
| defending one | 90 |
| settling a feud | 200 |
| a promo | 20, +45 if it lands at 70 or better |
| a season goal | 400 |
| finishing a career | 2,000 |
| daily objectives | 3 a day, 150 to 280 each, +250 for all three |
| the login streak | 120 to 320, with packs on days 4 and 7 |

A career year pays roughly 700 coins as a rookie and 2,300 at the top. A full
fifteen-year career lands near 20,000, which is about one legendary item.

**The day-to-day faucet is deliberately bigger than the career one.** A daily
player clears 600 to 900 coins a day without simming anything. That is the
part worth rewarding: coming back tomorrow, not simming ten years tonight.

### The prices

| rarity | was | now | in the catalogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| common | 1,200 | 3,000 | 9 |
| rare | 4,500 | 9,000 | 47 |
| epic | 12,000 | 24,000 | 39 |
| legendary | 30,000 | 60,000 | 32 |

Packs move the other way, because packs are supposed to be the route:

| pack | was | now | pulls | floor | pity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locker Room | 8,000 | 2,400 | 3 | common | epic within 5 |
| Main Event | 22,000 | 6,500 | 4 | rare | epic within 4 |
| Hall of Fame | 55,000 | 15,000 | 5 | epic | epic within 3 |

A base pack is 800 a pull against 3,000 to buy a common outright, so packs
run about four times cheaper per item. Buying the exact thing you want is a
premium you pay for certainty, and the forge (shards) is the patient route to
the same certainty. This is golf's rule and it is the right one.

### Existing saves

Coins are re-baselined to the starting wallet on load, once, via
`COIN_EPOCH`. **Owned gear and shards are kept.** Leaving an old balance
alone would mean a returning player owns the entire store on their first
visit and has nothing left to play for. The store says so in a panel rather
than letting somebody quietly discover they are broke.

## The Ropes Pass

A 60-day season, a 50-tier track, two lanes, season-scoped state that resets
when the season rolls so last season's rewards lock out.

Pass XP comes off the coin faucet at 0.5 per coin, so everything that pays
coins climbs it.

**The daily cap is the whole balance.** A career year can be simmed in about
two minutes. Without a cap the fastest route up the track is farming careers
in an evening, which is exactly the failure golf hit and had to write a long
comment about after a player finished all 60 tiers in five days. `PASS_XP_DAY`
caps a single day at 1,000 XP. The 50 tiers total 31,409 XP, so a perfectly
capped player needs 32 of the season's 60 days. A committed player finishes
around day 40; a casual one lands mid-track. The regression suite throws
200,000 coins of farming at it in one day and asserts the track barely moves.

The FREE lane is real: packs at 5, 15, 30 and 45, a cosmetic at 25, a shard
run, and a Main Event pack at the capstone. A player who never unlocks PRO
still has a track worth climbing.

**PRO, and the one thing to revisit.** Golf gates its premium lane behind a
$14.99 seasonal purchase and a server entitlement (`runtour_wallet().pass_active`).
The wrestling game has no account layer and no payment layer: it is
localStorage, single player, and unlisted. So PRO unlocks with coins, at
25,000, which is a real chase in year one and routine later. `PASS_PRO_PRICE`
is the constant to revisit when the shared account arrives. Nothing else in
this design assumes it stays a coin price.

## Dilemmas: what the scenes were missing

The scene engine already had multi-beat conversations with cast, memory and
effects. What golf's career mode has that these did not is that a choice
there is a **bet**, not a purchase. Three parts, all now present:

1. **Weighted outcomes.** An option carries `outcomes:[{p,tone,text,...}]` and
   the game rolls. `p` can be a function of the context, so being tough or
   smart genuinely improves the odds rather than skipping the roll. The same
   choice resolves several ways, so a player learns a shape instead of a
   lookup table.
2. **Temporary effects.** An outcome hands out `fx:{attr,amt,weeks,label}`.
   These move the actual attributes that `ovr()` and the sim read, they show
   on the hub as chips with a countdown, and they expire with an inbox note.
   A bad night is a bad month, not a ruined career.
3. **Arcs.** An outcome seeds `arc:{id, in:[3,6]}`, a follow-up scene that
   comes due weeks later and **outranks everything else in the deck**, because
   the game owes the player the other half of a decision they already made.

All three are opt-in. An option with a plain `eff` behaves exactly as before,
so nothing that already worked had to be rewritten.

Shipped with it: three dilemmas (the trainer's room and a shoulder you are
protecting, a card in a car park from a company that is not this one, three in
the morning with your own match on a laptop) and two arc payoffs (the first
night back from an injury you caused, and the office finding out you took the
card). The suite asserts every option has weighted outcomes, every outcome has
text and a positive weight, the roll actually spreads across 400 samples, an
effect bites and expires, and a seeded arc comes due, wins the pick and is
spent.

## What is not here

- **Real money.** No Stripe, no buckets, no server wallet. Golf's store is
  server-authoritative for a good reason: a client that can mint paid coins is
  a client that will. Adding purchases to a localStorage game would mean
  building the account layer first, and the brief says not to break the shared
  account integration.
- **A separate daily mode.** Golf's Daily Challenge is a whole game mode with
  its own leaderboard. The wrestling equivalent is objectives that tick during
  ordinary career play, which suits a game you play in bursts.
- **Boost gear.** Golf sells accessories that move skills, capped at +20. This
  game's store is cosmetics only, and the Merch Stand says so: skills are
  earned, never sold. Worth keeping.
