# Run The All-Stars: roster rights audit and what to add

Every one of the fifty four characters checked against US copyright and against
live trademarks, on 2 September 2026. The game is unlaunched, so the cheap time
to fix this is now.

Two separate things can go wrong and they need separate answers:

- **Copyright** covers a specific work and expires. US works published in 1930
  or earlier are in the public domain today, and one more year lapses each
  1 January.
- **Trademark** covers a name or a look used to sell things, does not expire
  while it is used, and survives the copyright by decades. Popeye's 1929 strip
  is free; the word POPEYE on a product is not.

A character can be safe on one and a problem on the other. The removals below
are for characters where the copyright itself has not run out.

## Removed

| Character | Why | Free on |
|---|---|---|
| Dick Tracy | Chester Gould, 1931. Not public domain yet. Live trademark, Tribune Content Agency. | 1 Jan 2027 |
| Flash Gordon | Alex Raymond, 1934. King Features licenses it actively. | 1 Jan 2030 |
| Tintin | Hergé, 1929, Belgian. EU term is life plus seventy and Hergé died in 1983. Moulinsart enforces aggressively. | 2054 |
| Felix the Cat | The 1919 to 1928 shorts have lapsed, but the character is a live registered trademark owned by a major studio, and the design is the trademark. | trademark, no expiry |

Dick Tracy is four months away and can come back on 1 January 2027 if you want
him. The other three are not close.

## Replaced with

Five public domain characters chosen to fill the same roles rather than to
resemble the departed. Four out, five in, so the roster is 55.

| New | Source | Fills | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paul Bunyan** | American folk, printed 1916 | Flash Gordon | Power |
| **Pinocchio** | Collodi, 1883, NOT the Disney design | Felix the Cat | Wild |
| **The Golem** | Jewish folklore, Prague | Dick Tracy | Power and Defense |
| **Ichabod Crane** | Irving, 1820 | Tintin, on the beat and in the press box | Contact |
| **Long John Silver** | Stevenson, 1883 | the second press seat | Balanced |

Paul Bunyan swings an axe handle and is the one power hitter with no speed
at all. The Golem does exactly what the lineup card says, which is both the
joke and the highest defence rating on the roster. Pinocchio is made of
wood, so nothing about him tires or bruises; he is drawn with the marionette
joints and the grain showing and **without the long nose**, which was never
the interesting half of him. Ichabod Crane pairs with the Headless Horseman
the roster already had, and reads the pitcher like a lesson.

Long John Silver takes the newsdesk in the press room, from The Spyglass,
which is his own inn in the novel. Three of the twenty desk lines assumed a
modern newsroom (a tape on a desk, filing a piece) and were rewritten for a
man with a log rather than a deadline. Ichabod Crane takes the second seat
for The Hollow Gazette: the lines there are all notebooks, spare pencils and
filing the wrong ending, which is a schoolmaster to the letter.

Nobody in the five carries a quirk. Casey's `mudville` quirk left with him,
along with the branch in `resolveSwing` and its entry in the posture guard's
allow list, because a quirk key no roster row carries is dead code the guard
still has to permit.

## Redesigned, not removed

| Character | What was wrong | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Frankenstein | Flat top, neck bolts and green skin are Jack Pierce's 1931 makeup for Universal, who assert rights in it. | Shelley's 1818 creature: yellow skin, lustrous flowing black hair, no bolts |
| The Witch | Green skin is Margaret Hamilton's 1939 MGM makeup. No witch before that film is green, and Baum's is not green in the 1900 text. | Ashen and drawn, the older folkloric hag |
| King Kong | The 1933 film is under copyright until 2029. *Universal City Studios v. Nintendo* (1984) found Universal had itself asserted the Kong story was public domain, but KING KONG is a live trademark. | Renamed **The Great Ape**. Same giant ape, no trademark |
| Dorothy | Ruby slippers are MGM's. | Silver shoes, per the 1900 book |
| Peter Pan | Green tunic, cap and belt are the 1953 film's. | Barrie's leaf-clad Peter, barefoot |
| Pooh | The red shirt arrived on a 1932 licence. | Shepard's 1926 bare bear with a honey pot |

The last three shipped earlier today.

## Kept, with a note

- **Popeye** entered the US public domain on 1 January 2025 (the 1929 Thimble
  Theatre strips). King Features retains the trademark, so the name on
  merchandise is a different question from the character in a game. The spinach
  gimmick is a 1931 story element and is also free.
- **Sherlock Holmes**: every Conan Doyle work has been public domain in the US
  since 1 January 2023.
- **Arsène Lupin**: Leblanc, 1905, and Leblanc died in 1941.
- **Dracula**, **The Invisible Man**, **Mr Hyde**, **Alice**, **The Scarecrow**,
  **The Cowardly Lion**, **Tom Sawyer**, **Huck Finn**: all nineteenth century
  or turn of the century, all clear. The Invisible Man's bandages and goggles
  are in Wells's 1897 text, not only in the 1933 film.
- **Peter Pan** is public domain in the US. In the UK, Great Ormond Street
  Hospital holds a perpetual right to royalties under a 1988 statute. That is a
  royalty right on performances and publications, not a copyright, and it does
  not reach a US game.
- Everything folkloric (Santa, Krampus, the Yeti, the Chupacabra, Medusa,
  the Phoenix, Baba Yaga's cousins) is free and always was.

### Do not add

| Character | Why |
|---|---|
| Tarzan | 1912 novel is public domain but Edgar Rice Burroughs Inc. holds and defends the trademark |
| Zorro | Same shape: the 1919 story lapsed, Zorro Productions enforces the mark |
| The Shadow | 1931, not free until 2027, and trademarked |
| Doc Savage | 1933 |
| Buck Rogers | The 1928 story is free, but the estate litigates and the name is a mark |
| Betty Boop | Fleischer 1930, trademark actively enforced |
| Mickey Mouse | The 1928 *Steamboat Willie* design only, and using it invites exactly the argument you do not want |

Buck Rogers and Betty Boop are the two that look safe on a copyright date and
are not, which is the pattern worth remembering: when a name is still selling
merchandise, the date is not the whole answer.

## The migration this needed

Removing a character breaks a save that drafted them. A saved season stores nine
roster KEYS, and `State.team.map(k => ROSTER_BY_KEY[k])` hands back `undefined`
for a key that no longer exists, which throws at the first name lookup with the
whole game screen already up. `repairLineup()` swaps any unknown key for an
unlocked stand-in, in a fixed order so the same save repairs the same way every
time, and it runs both in `migrateSeason` and again in `startGame` for the
opponent lineups.
