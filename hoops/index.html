Run The Floor

Spin for a season and a club. Draft six. Play 82 and chase a ring.

How a run goes six spins, one cap

The wheel gives you a club and a season. You sign one player off that roster, and you do it six times to fill a point guard, a shooting guard, a small forward, a power forward, a centre and a sixth man. Every signing comes out of a $135M cap, and the cap is the whole game: the best man on every board runs to about $300M, so it cannot be done.

Players who really did share a club, a college or a draft class build chemistry, which is worth a few points of net rating. Then you play all 82 games against real teams out of the same data, and if you finish well enough you get a run at a title.

How to play

Why six stars lose the thinking behind the game

The ball only bounces once

An NBA team takes about 88 shots a night, and a starting five plus a sixth man accounts for roughly 72 of them. Every player here carries the shots he actually took. Draft six men who each took twenty a night on their own team and you have a roster asking for 120 shots that do not exist. Jordan, Harden, Bryant, Malone and O'Neal is the most talented lineup you can assemble in this game and one of the worst basketball teams in it.

Somebody has to be able to shoot

Without spacing the paint is a car park. But the three-point line did not exist until 1980, and Jerry West attempted none in 1972 because nobody could, so shooting is measured against what a player's own league shot, on two counts: how many he took, and what share of his attempts they were. Steve Kerr took only 2.9 a night, which is unremarkable, and they were half his shots, which is not.

An anchor, not a committee

Five men with 0.4 blocks each do not add up to a rim protector; one man with two of them is one. Same for passing: a roster whose best creator averages two assists is five players taking turns. Both read the best man on the roster rather than the total, which is why signing Ben Wallace or Magic Johnson changes a team in a way that five willing helpers never does.

Then it tells you what you built

When the sixth man signs, the game names the system your roster plays: Showtime, the Triangle, Grit and Grind, Pick and Roll, Twin Towers, Moreyball, Point Centre and the rest. They are checked against real lineups, so the 1996 Bulls come back running the Triangle and the 1989 Pistons come back Grit and Grind. Get it wrong and it will tell you that too: six volume scorers are not a system, they are Too Many Mouths. The full rules are here.
