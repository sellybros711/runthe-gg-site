How to Play The Perfect Season

Six spins, six signings, one salary cap and a full 17-game season. This is the complete guide to the rules, the maths behind your roster rating, and the strategy that separates a 9-8 team from a 20-0 one.

The game in one paragraph

The Perfect Season is a free browser game about building the best six-player NFL roster you can from the whole history of the league. You don't pick who you want — a wheel gives you a year, then a team from that year, and you sign one player off that roster. Do that six times, stay under a $140M cap, then play out a 17-game regular season and, if you're good enough, the playoffs. There is nothing to download and no account needed to play a solo run.

The wheel: years and teams

Every signing starts with two spins. The first lands on a season. The second lands on one of the teams that played in that season. You then choose any player from that specific team in that specific year — the 1985 Bears, the 2007 Patriots, the 1994 49ers, whatever the wheel hands you.

This is the part of the game that rewards actual football knowledge. Two players can spin the same year and the same team and walk away with very different signings, because knowing which player on a given roster was at their peak that season — and what they cost — is the whole skill. A team's best-known name is not always its best value.

The $140M salary cap

You have $140M to fill six roster spots. Every player carries a price derived from how good they were in the season you're drafting them from, so a peak-year superstar can eat a third of your budget on his own.

Leaving money unspent is a mistake, not a safety net. There is no prize for finishing under budget. If you end the draft with $20M in your pocket, that is $20M of roster quality you simply chose not to buy.

The interesting tension is that the cap is generous enough for two or three genuinely elite players, but not for six. Every run turns into the same question: do you spend big early and scrape the bottom later, or spread the money and end up with a roster of very good players and no great ones? Both approaches win seasons. They don't win the same way.

Re-spins and what they cost

If the draw is unusable you can re-spin the year, or re-spin the team, instead of signing someone you don't want. Re-spins are not free — they come straight off your remaining cap space on a rising ladder:

Re-spinCostWhat it means
First$5MCheap. Worth it to escape a genuinely barren roster.
Second$10MNow you're paying a starter's salary to reroll.
Third$15MExpensive enough that it should be a last resort.

Re-spinning three times costs $30M — over a fifth of your cap — before you've signed anybody with it. The discipline of the game is knowing when a mediocre draw is still better than the money it takes to escape it.

Chemistry

Players who share history lift each other. If two of your signings played on the same team, went to the same college, or came out of the same draft class, they build chemistry, and chemistry raises the rating of the whole roster rather than just the pair.

This quietly changes how you value a spin. A slightly worse player who links to three guys you've already signed can be worth more than the better player who links to nobody. Late in a draft, when four spots are filled, chemistry is often the single biggest lever left — and it's the reason experienced players start tracking colleges and draft classes from the very first signing rather than the fourth.

Roster shape and scheme fit

Talent alone does not win. The game grades the shape of what you've built, and a badly shaped roster wastes the talent on it.

  • A quarterback who can't throw makes your receivers worthless. Elite pass-catchers with no one to get them the ball are a wasted signing, not an insurance policy.
  • Stars with nobody around them get found out. One 99-rated player and five replacement-level bodies loses to six good ones more often than people expect.
  • The roster you end up with is read as an offensive scheme, and a roster that clearly commits to one identity earns a fit bonus on top of its raw rating.

The fit bonus is deliberately modest — a small percentage, scaled by how strongly your roster actually commits to a scheme. It is a tiebreaker between two good rosters, not a shortcut past a better one.

The season and the playoffs

Once the roster is locked you play all 17 games. Each one is simulated against a real historical opponent, with a live drive-by-drive broadcast and a score bug that changes era with the year you're playing in.

ResultWhat happens
12+ winsYou're in the playoffs.
15+ winsYou're in and you skip a round on a bye.
Under 12Season over. Start again.
Any playoff lossThe run ends immediately — there is no second chance.

Win every game in the regular season and every game in the playoffs and you finish 20-0. That is the perfect season the game is named after, and it is genuinely rare — the home screen keeps a live count of how many have ever been recorded across all players.

One Franchise mode

One Franchise is the same game with the team wheel locked. You pick a franchise, and from then on only the year moves — so every player you sign is someone who actually wore that jersey. It turns the game into a question of building the greatest all-time version of one specific team.

Each franchise keeps its own separate leaderboard, so you're ranked against other people building the same team rather than against the whole site. One Franchise needs a free RunThe.GG account, because the run has to be saved somewhere to be ranked.

Seven strategy tips

  • Sign your quarterback early. The position gates the value of everything else you buy, and finding out late that you can't afford one is how good drafts die.
  • Learn the cheap peak years. A great player one season before or after their famous year is often dramatically cheaper for a fraction less production.
  • Track colleges and draft classes from signing one. Chemistry is easiest to build when you're still choosing between many options, not when you have one slot left.
  • Treat the first re-spin as basically free and the third as basically never. $5M is a rounding error; $15M is a starter.
  • Spend the last dollar. Finishing with cap space is the most common unforced error in the game.
  • Commit to a scheme. A roster that clearly wants to run the ball beats a roster that half-wants to do everything.
  • Remember 15 wins buys a bye. A playoff run is one fewer coin-flip if you clear 15 in the regular season, so a marginal roster upgrade is worth more than it looks.

Common questions

Is The Perfect Season free?

Yes, completely. The site is funded by advertising, and there is no paid version, no subscription and no paywall in front of any mode.

Do I need an account?

Not to play a solo season. A free RunThe.GG account saves your runs, puts you on the global leaderboards, and unlocks One Franchise mode. The same account works across every game on the site.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. The game runs in any modern mobile browser with no app to install. You can add RunThe.GG to your Home Screen if you'd like it to open full screen.

Where do the players and ratings come from?

Ratings are derived from publicly available historical NFL statistics, season by season, so a player's card reflects the year you draft them from rather than their career as a whole.
